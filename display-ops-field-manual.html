How your display ad money is actually made

A plain-English map of the stack, the levers you control, the assets you're selling, and the metrics that decide who wins — written so Kelly & Jake can walk into the NextMillennium meeting knowing exactly what to push on.

Ad server · Google Ad Manager (GAM) Remnant partner · NextMillennium House fallback · AvantLink 2026 Prepared · Jul 2026
01

The stack — who gets first crack at each ad slot

Every time a page loads, each ad slot runs a mini-auction inside GAM. GAM fills it in priority order: guaranteed deals you sold directly go first, then programmatic demand competes on price for what's left, and a house ad mops up anything still empty. This order is the single most important thing to understand — it's literally a waterfall.

Direct-sold sponsorships

GuaranteedGAM priority 4–8 · highest

The sponsor ads you sell yourself (endemic overland brands). These are booked to deliver a promised number of impressions, so GAM serves them before anyone else. Highest revenue per impression, but capped by how much you've sold.

NextMillennium (programmatic remnant)

Auction / priceGAM priority 12 · competes on CPM

Whatever direct-sold didn't take becomes unsold / remnant inventory. NextMillennium brings a pile of buyers (SSPs/DSPs) who bid on it in real time; the highest bid wins. This is where "fill rate" and "CPM" live — and where the video-fill problem sits, because a display slot doesn't invite dedicated video budgets.

AvantLink 2026 (house affiliate)

House · % goalcurrently taking 20% of unsold

Your own affiliate creative, running as a house campaign. It earns on clicks/conversions, not on impressions. Right now it's set to grab 20% of unsold inventory — the exact lever you're asking about in Section 05.

The one sentence that ties it together

Direct-sold is capped by your sales effort; the house ad is a fallback; NextMillennium is the engine that monetizes the 80–95% of inventory in the middle — so almost everything in this manual is about feeding that engine more and better inventory.

02

Your assets — what you're actually selling

You don't sell "ads." You sell attention on specific inventory. Knowing your assets tells you where the upside is and gives you leverage in the NM conversation.

Inventory

The ad slots

Each placement (leaderboard, in-content rectangle, sidebar, sticky anchor) × each page = an impression you can sell. Their sizes, position, and viewability set their value.

Traffic

Pageviews & sessions

The forum is the sleeper asset: long sessions and huge pageview volume make it ideal for ad refresh (Section 04) — more monetizable impressions from the same visit.

Audience

Endemic off-road buyers

A high-intent, gear-buying niche. Worth premium CPMs to endemic advertisers and the reason direct deals and video budgets are realistic — this audience is a scarce buy.

First-party data

Newsletter & logged-in forum

256K newsletter subscribers and logged-in forum users are addressable identity in a cookieless world — fuel for higher match rates and CPMs if wired into the demand stack.

Content

XOVERLAND owned video

Real video content is the asset that unlocks a true video unit (instream pre-roll), the highest-CPM inventory you could add. Most sites can only run junky outstream; you can run your own footage.

Authorization

Your ads.txt file

The public file at expeditionportal.com/ads.txt that says which sellers may sell you. If NM's seats aren't listed, that demand gets filtered out before it can bid.

03

The levers — what you can actually change

Optimization is just pulling these knobs and measuring the effect on Session RPM (Section 04). They split into three groups: the inventory you offer, the demand competing for it, and how the waterfall is configured.

Inventory levers — mostly yours to pull

Ad refresh ★ biggest volume lever

Reload an ad after it's been viewed for 30–60s of active on-screen time. On long forum sessions this can multiply monetizable impressions. Must be viewability-triggered so you don't tank CPMs.

Placement density & position

More slots and better positions = more impressions, but watch UX, viewability, and Core Web Vitals. Adding one well-placed in-content unit often beats three bad ones.

Sizes offered

Enable high-value sizes: 300×600 half-page and 970×250 billboard command far higher CPMs than a lone 300×250. Multi-size slots let more buyers compete.

Lazy loading

Only request an ad as the slot scrolls near view. Raises viewability (you're not counting ads nobody saw), which directly lifts CPMs and Core Web Vitals.

Sticky / anchor units

A pinned anchor (bottom of screen) or sticky sidebar stays in view longer → higher viewability → higher CPM. Also the natural home for the outstream video player.

A dedicated video unit

The incremental play: a sticky outstream player (or instream with your own footage) turns a slot into a true video impression that dedicated video budgets ($15–40+ CPM) will bid on.

Demand levers — you direct, NM executes

Number of demand partners

How many SSPs/bidders compete in the auction (header bidding depth + Open Bidding). More competition = higher clearing price. Ask NM exactly who's live.

Price floors

The minimum you'll accept. Too high kills fill; too low leaves money on the table. Should be dynamic, set per size/geo/device — a common source of quiet under-earning.

Identity modules

UID2 / ID5 / LiveRamp / SharedID let buyers recognize users without third-party cookies. Big driver of both fill and CPM as cookies go away.

ads.txt completeness

Every NM seat and reseller line must be authorized, or that demand is dropped. A 5-minute audit that can meaningfully move fill.

Configuration levers — inside GAM

House / AvantLink priority & share ★ your open question

Whether AvantLink sits above or below NM in the waterfall, and what % it takes. This decides if the 20%→5% change actually hands impressions to NM. See Section 05.

Direct-sold commitment

How many impressions your direct deals reserve. Over-booking guaranteed deals starves the NM auction; under-booking leaves premium money unsold.

04

The metrics — the scoreboard, in plain English

You'll hear these constantly. The gold-starred ones are the numbers that actually decide whether a change made you money. Optimize Session RPM above all — it's the only number that captures fill, price, and ad count at once.

Impressions

The number of ads served.

Raw volume. Meaningless on its own — a million cheap impressions can earn less than ten thousand good ones.

Fill rate

Of all ad requests, the % that returned a paying ad. filled ÷ requested

Your "low video fill" problem lives here: video ad requests are mostly coming back empty because a display slot doesn't attract video buyers.

CPMcost per mille

What an advertiser pays per 1,000 impressions.

The price of a win. Video CPMs run several times display CPMs — which is why a real video unit is worth the effort.

eCPMeffective CPM

Blended revenue per 1,000 impressions across filled and unfilled requests.

The honest apples-to-apples number for comparing two setups — because it bakes in fill rate, not just winning price.

Session RPM ★ north star

Revenue per 1,000 sessions (or pageviews = Page RPM).

The one number to optimize. It captures fill × CPM × ads-per-session together, so it tells you if a change actually grew the pie or just reshuffled it.

Viewability

% of impressions actually seen (industry standard: 50% of pixels on screen for 1s+).

Buyers pay more for ads people see. Lazy-loading and sticky units raise it; it's an indirect but strong lever on CPM.

Video fill / VCR

Video-specific fill rate and Video Completion Rate (how often the ad plays to the end).

The KPIs that will prove a dedicated video unit is working. Track these separately from display from day one.

Core Web VitalsLCP · CLS · INP

Google's page-experience scores for load speed, layout stability, and responsiveness.

Ads are the usual culprit for bad scores, and bad scores hurt SEO and traffic — the guardrail on every "add more ads" lever.

05

Your AvantLink question — 20% → 5%, worked out

"Will dropping AvantLink from 20% to 5% of unsold inventory increase NextMillennium earnings?" The honest answer: it depends entirely on one thing you can check in GAM in two minutes — where AvantLink sits in the waterfall relative to NM. There are two cases, and they point opposite ways.

Case A — likely worth it

AvantLink is ABOVE NM in priority

If the AvantLink line item is a Sponsorship or Standard type (priority 4–10), it skims 20% of impressions off the top before NM ever competes. Cutting to 5% hands roughly 15% more impressions to the NM auction. Worth it if NM earns more on those than AvantLink does.

Case B — no effect on NM

AvantLink is BELOW NM (true "house")

If AvantLink is a House line item (priority 16), NM already gets first look and AvantLink only fills what NM declined. Cutting it won't feed NM anything — those impressions were NM passbacks. You'd just create blanks. The lever is moot for NM here.

How to decide, regardless of case. Don't argue theory — compare revenue-per-1,000-impressions. AvantLink pays on conversions, so convert its affiliate earnings into an effective impression value and put it next to NM's eCPM on the same slot:

# AvantLink's true value as a display unit AvantLink eCPM = (affiliate $ earned ÷ impressions shown) × 1000 # Give NM the impressions only if NM eCPM > AvantLink eCPM → shift the 15% to NM # Net gain from the change (per 1,000 impressions moved) Δ = NM eCPM − AvantLink eCPM

Why NM probably wins — but verify. An affiliate creative in a display slot usually converts at a low enough rate that its eCPM is a fraction of programmatic CPM. So Case A most likely favors NM. But "probably" isn't a number — pull AvantLink's actual earnings and impressions for that placement and compute its eCPM before you commit.

The clean way to settle it

Run it as a controlled test: drop AvantLink to 5% for 2 weeks, then compare Session RPM before vs. after (RPM, not raw dollars, to cancel out traffic swings). Net win = the rise in NM revenue minus any drop in AvantLink revenue. If GAM's A/B experiments are available, split-test it instead of a before/after. Either way, let the RPM decide — not the theory.
