Expedition Portal · NextMillennium 2023 → 2026

The revenue didn't erode. It was switched off.

NM earnings fell 73% from 2023 to today. With 2025 now in hand, the decline decomposes into three fixable mechanisms — the forum removed from scope, a broken ads.txt, and a mobile layout nobody configured — which means most of it is recoverable without adding a single ad format the community would object to.

01

Three years, one direction

2023–2025 are actuals from the NM exports. 2026 is annualized from Jan 1–Jul 22 ($5,672 over 203 days). The 2025 file closes the earlier data gap — and shows 2024→2025 as the single largest drop (−$12,356, −48%), with a composition that changed exactly when the ads.txt file broke.

02

The 2023→2024 drop was inventory, not yield

This is the finding that changes the strategy. If yield had degraded, fill rates and eCPMs would have fallen. They didn't — they held to within a fraction of a percent, while volume halved on exactly two placements. That is the signature of pages being removed from a placement's eligible inventory.

The forum-shaped hole

Every eCPM was flat or up in 2024, and the blended rate rose from $2.00 to $2.15. Revenue still fell $11,519 — because gunstar and eagle alone gave up $13,174 on lost volume. NM confirmed on the call that they were asked to exclude forum pages, and Brian confirmed the reason: community complaints about banners injected between posts. That decision is the single largest identifiable revenue event in this data.

One check against the obvious alternative explanation: if site traffic had simply collapsed, every placement's requests would have fallen together. Instead prometheus requests rose 143% in the same year. Traffic decline doesn't fit; scope removal does.

Yield: the dated mechanism

eCPM steps down exactly at the ads.txt break

The stale file was stamped 12-18-2024. Non-video eCPM: $2.11 with the healthy file (2024) → $1.72 the first full year on the broken file (2025) → $1.58 (2026). 56 missing seller records filtered demand for 19 months. Fixed Jul 26 — August data shows whether the climb back toward $2 begins.

Volume: a multi-year slide

Non-video requests: 37.5M → 28.5M → 20.1M → 10.2M

With 2025 in, the volume loss is a steady four-year slide, not one event — so it's a mix: some traffic trend, some loader gating, some layout. The controllable parts are the loader's scroll gate and the mobile template; the uncontrollable part is traffic itself.

The mobile factor

56% of users get a layout nobody configured

Measured Jul 31: on mobile, 29 of 30 article ad units render after the article ends; two forum slots serve desktop-width creatives clipped off a 375px screen. Mostly-unseen units drag viewability, and buyers price that in — Safari clears $0.76 against the desktop-led blend. The web-dev ticket fixes this at the template level.

Honest caveat

Traffic data still splits the blame

Without GA4 sessions for 2023–2026, I can't apportion the request slide between gating, layout, and genuine traffic decline. If sessions fell materially, part of the volume loss isn't recoverable by config. Pull sessions by year before committing to a forecast.

Why this is good news

Replacing the current in-banner video calls costs at most ~$2,400/year — and that revenue would largely be re-earned by a real pre-roll unit at far higher CPMs. The 2025 file dates the bloat: requests jumped 5.4× in 2025 and another 4.6× in 2026 while revenue stayed flat near $1.9K — 39× the 2023 volume for the same money. Pure overhead dragging every blended metric. The pre-roll direction agreed on the call (own content, five seconds, skippable) is strictly better than what's running now, and it removes an unmeasured Connatix layer at the same time.

The strategic point

Baseline $10.2K plus the conservative end of each item lands near $23K; the optimistic end approaches $50K. 2023 was $37,356. Getting back to the historical peak sits inside that range without adopting a single high-impact format — no sticky mobile anchors, no interstitials, no in-flow forum units, no follow-you video. That is the answer to NM's push toward "higher impact formats": we don't need them to hit the number.

06

Guardrails — what stays off the table

Declining

Formats that caused the problem

  • Units injected between forum posts — the original complaint
  • Sticky mobile anchor units
  • Video players that float, follow, or expand on scroll
  • Interstitials and full-screen takeovers
Accepting

Formats with an honest trade

  • Sidebar and below-content forum units, lazy-loaded, per-page capped
  • Pre-roll on our own video — 5s, skippable, on content the reader chose
  • Backfill in existing GAM slots — fills what's already empty
  • Viewport-triggered loading rather than eager loading
One recommendation to push back on

NM's position is that ads should load "as quickly as possible" — that's their yield incentive, and it costs page speed. The right resolution isn't the current scroll-count gate or eager loading: it's viewport-based lazy loading, where a slot requests an ad as it approaches the screen. That recovers the lost auctions without the Core Web Vitals penalty, and it raises viewability, which lifts CPMs on its own.

07

Sequence

  1. Measure the ads.txt fix

    Compare August eCPM against June. This is a free read on whether the 12-18-2024 breakage explains the yield half of the decline — and it calibrates every other estimate here.

  2. Pull the data that resizes the model

    GA4 sessions for 2023–2026; NM viewability + eCPM split by device and placement; GAM unfilled impressions by ad unit; on-site video page traffic. These convert most of the wide ranges above into real numbers. (The 2025 NM report is now in — it confirmed the ads.txt timing.)

  3. Install the GAM backfill tag

    Behind direct-sold, ahead of AvantLink. Zero UX cost, and it finally puts a paid layer under the inventory that fell to house fill when Montana completed. This is also what makes the AvantLink 20%→5% question meaningful at last.

  4. Playwire migration, staged and previewed

    Dev stages the preview build; review player behavior against the guardrails above before production. Verify no floating or expanding video behavior ships with it.

  5. Re-enable the forum, guardrails first

    Deploy the forum ads.txt, then turn NM on with sidebar and below-content placements only. Watch complaint volume and Session RPM together for two weeks — the community reaction is a metric here, not an afterthought.

  6. Ship the mobile ticket + loader timing

    Forum thread-slot size mappings (P1 — clipped ads are billing advertisers for unreadable impressions), mobile cap on the sidebar generator, 2–3 in-content units on mobile articles, lazy loading + collapseEmptyDivs, and viewport-triggered loading in place of the scroll-count gate. Measure request volume, viewability and Core Web Vitals before and after.

  7. Video pre-roll on owned content

    Once Aaron's integration details land. Retire the in-banner video calls at the same time — at ~$2.4K/yr they are not worth protecting, and running both would double the request overhead.
